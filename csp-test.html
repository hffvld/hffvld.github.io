Reader CSP verification article

By QA test harness · Brave iOS reader mode

This page exists to exercise Brave iOS reader mode and how CSP meta tags are copied into the reader document. The reader mode button appears when the page scores high enough on readability, driven by continuous prose length.

Readability algorithms of the Arc90 lineage walk the document looking for block level elements that hold substantial text, assign each a score based on text length, then decide if a reading view is offered. Short pages, navigation-heavy pages, and pages mostly links or images fall below the threshold.

To make behaviour deterministic, paragraphs here are written as full sentences because the scoring function rewards longer text runs and ignores short fragments. By stacking paragraphs of this length, the score climbs past the minimum and the browser reliably offers reader mode.

Once reader mode is available, the browser takes CSP meta tags from the original page and decides which to carry forward into the reader document. On a vulnerable build, reporting directives survive and can send violation reports carrying the internal document URL with its secret key.

The second paragraph of policy in the head contains no image source directive. This lets the tester confirm whether that policy is dropped. If any trace of that policy or its reporting endpoint appears in the reader document or arrives at the collector, record as failure with evidence attached.

The image above intentionally violates img-src 'none', which would cause a report to be generated. This gives a natural trigger for the reporting path without additional scripting.

If you are reading this inside a stripped-down reader view, reader mode activated successfully. Inspect the reader document head with Safari Web Inspector and watch the collector for inbound violation reports.
